A landscaper who starts with the ground

Ipswich sits on two very different soils. East of town, towards Kesgrave and Martlesham, the ground is light and sandy and drains fast. North and west, towards Whitton and Sproughton, you are more likely to hit heavy clay that holds water through winter. That difference decides how deep we dig, what sub-base goes under a patio and which plants will thrive. We check it on the site visit, before we price anything.

Most of our work is in ordinary family gardens: a tired lawn that needs re-levelling, a patio that has sunk, a fence that went over in the last storm. We also take on full redesigns for new-build plots and older properties where nothing has been touched in decades. If you want the detail on each service, see our landscaping services in Ipswich.

When you hire a landscaper, you are really paying for the work you cannot see once the job is finished. The depth of the dig, the compaction of the sub-base, the fall on a patio and the drainage behind a wall decide whether a garden still looks right in ten years. We photograph that stage of every job so you can see what went in underneath.

We keep the team small on purpose. The person who measures your garden is involved in building it, so nothing gets lost between the quote and the work, and you always know who to speak to.

What to think about before hiring a landscaper in Ipswich

Start with how you use the garden

Before talking materials, think about who uses the garden and when. A family with young children needs a robust lawn and a safe, level patio. Someone who entertains wants a larger seating area close to the kitchen. If you work from home, a quiet corner or a spot for a garden office may matter more than a big lawn. We ask about this first, because it shapes every decision after it.

Check where the sun falls

Ipswich gardens vary a lot in orientation. A south-facing garden in Rushmere gets sun all day and suits a patio at the far end as well as by the house. A north-facing terraced garden near the town centre may only get evening sun at the back, which is often the best place for seating. We map sun and shade at the site visit and position patios and planting to match.

Plan drainage early

Rain has to go somewhere. A large new patio replaces ground that used to soak up water, and on clay soil that water can end up pooling against the house or flooding the lawn. Building in falls, channel drains or a soakaway at the start costs far less than fixing problems later.

Think about access

Many Ipswich terraces and semis have no side access, so everything going in and out has to come through the house or over a fence. We plan for this at the quote stage, with protective sheeting, barrow routes or compact machinery, so there are no surprises on the first day.

Phase the work if you need to

You do not have to do everything at once. A common approach is to do the groundwork, drainage and patio first, then lawns and planting in a later season. We can design the whole garden up front so each phase fits the final plan.

What affects the cost of landscaping

Every garden is priced individually, but these are the factors that move the price most.

Size and access
Bigger areas need more materials and labour, and gardens with no side access take longer to build.
Materials
Porcelain and natural stone cost more than concrete slabs or gravel. Composite decking costs more than softwood.
Groundwork
Breaking out old concrete, levelling slopes and adding drainage all take time before anything visible goes in.
Waste removal
Old patios and large volumes of soil mean more skips or grab-lorry loads.
Planting
Mature plants and trees cost more than young stock but give an instant finished look.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a landscaper in Ipswich cost?

It depends on the size of the garden, the materials and how much groundwork the site needs. A small turfing job and a full garden rebuild sit at very different ends. We price every job from a site visit and give you a fixed written figure, so the number you agree is the number you pay.

Do I need planning permission for a patio or fence?

Usually not. Fences up to 2 metres high, or 1 metre next to a road, and most back-garden patios fall under permitted development. Front-garden paving over 5 square metres must be permeable or drain to a lawn or border, otherwise it needs permission. Listed buildings and conservation areas have extra rules, and we will flag it if your property is affected.

When is the best time of year for landscaping?

Hard landscaping can go ahead most of the year. Turfing and planting do best in autumn and spring, when the ground is moist and not frozen. If you want the garden ready for summer, book a site visit in late winter.

Do you take away the waste?

Yes. Spoil, old slabs and green waste are removed and disposed of properly, and the cost is included in your quote.

Do you offer garden maintenance after the build?

Our main focus is building gardens. After handover we give you a care sheet covering watering, mowing and how to look after your patio or deck, and we are happy to come back for seasonal tidy-ups or repairs.

Can you work on sloping gardens?

Yes. Sloping gardens are common around Ipswich, particularly near the river valleys. We use retaining walls, steps and terracing to create flat, usable areas, with drainage behind every wall.

How far ahead do I need to book?

Spring and early summer are the busiest times. Booking your site visit a few months ahead gives you the best choice of start dates, but smaller jobs can sometimes be fitted in sooner.

Do I need to be home while you work?

Not usually. We need access to the garden, an outside tap and somewhere to plug in tools. Most clients are at work while we are on site, and we send a short update at the end of each day.
